Hackathon Problem Statement Response — Disaster Search & Rescue Drone

1. Executive Summary
AEGIS-SAR is a decentralized swarm of edge-autonomous drones that goes beyond the standard RGB-and-thermal detection brief. Four independent sensing modalities — vision, thermal, acoustic, and chemical — are fused through a hybrid deterministic-plus-machine-learning pipeline, and the swarm coordinates over a bandwidth-starved mesh protocol that lets multiple drones build one shared disaster map without any cloud or cellular link.
The central design principle is a triage-and-trust architecture: every detection carries a deterministic confidence audit trail, so responders can see why the system flagged something rather than trusting an opaque bounding box.

4. Hybrid Deterministic + ML Hazard Classification
Pure black-box classification is fragile when a disaster scene does not resemble training data. AEGIS-SAR scores every hazard through two independent paths that must agree — or the disagreement itself becomes signal.
Deterministic checks — Fire: thermal hotspot above 250°C combined with 1–3 Hz IR flicker and RGB motion texture.
Floodwater: reflectivity signature plus a digital-elevation-model delta between pre-disaster and current altitude-corrected depth.
Downed power line: linear thermal anomaly near mapped grid infrastructure (offline OSM layer).
Structural instability: vibration signature during close hover plus thermal-creep pattern across repeated passes.
Fusion rule: agreement between the ML and deterministic paths produces a high-confidence, auto-flagged alert. Disagreement is routed to a “needs human review” queue with both reasoning traces shown on the dashboard — an auditable reason for every alert.
5. Multi-Sensor Victim Detection
Acoustic layer — 4-mic MEMS array with onboard beamforming to localize cries, voices, or tapping from survivors occluded by rubble; a lightweight CNN on mel-spectrograms separates human vocalizations from wind, water, or machinery.
Gas/VOC layer — MOX sensor array (CO, CH4, VOC) with deterministic threshold logic and temperature/humidity drift compensation.
Thermal + RGB fusion — Cross-attention fusion of LWIR thermal blobs with RGB context, distinguishing a live human thermal signature from a heat-retaining rock or debris pile.
Through-rubble vital-sign radar — FMCW/UWB radar performing micro-Doppler analysis of chest displacement to detect breathing and heartbeat through non-metallic debris — the one modality here that no camera or microphone can replicate.
6. Navigation: Graceful Degradation
Normal conditions — GPS/RTK plus visual-inertial SLAM (ORB-SLAM3-lite, quantized) for centimeter-level outdoor localization.
GPS-denied — Visual SLAM takes over for urban canyons and indoor collapse.
Vision-denied (smoke/dust/darkness) — Deterministic dead-reckoning — IMU double-integration corrected by optical flow on thermal imagery (which still registers in smoke/dust) plus barometric altitude hold, with drift bounded by ToF-ring wall-following.
7. Swarm Coordination and Offline Map Fusion
Mesh protocol — ESP-NOW for tight-formation drone-to-drone links (under 50 m, low latency); LoRa for longer-range relay.
Decentralized SLAM merging — Drones share compact pose-graph keyframes, not raw point clouds; any drone or the dashboard can merge overlapping keyframes via loop-closure detection, so the shared map has no single point of failure.
Adaptive coverage planning — An ant-colony-inspired algorithm biases the swarm's search toward regions with rising acoustic/thermal signal density.
Bandwidth-efficient telemetry — Each detection serializes to a ~200-byte packet, sendable over LoRa even in a fully offline zone; full imagery queues locally and syncs opportunistically over Wi-Fi/5G.
8. Victim Triage Score Engine
Every detected person receives a transparent, computed score rather than a bare “person detected” flag:
TriageScore = w1·(thermal_confidence) + w2·(acoustic_confirmation) + w3·(proximity_to_hazard) + w4·(structural_risk_nearby) + w5·(time_since_last_contact_estimate)
Weights are configurable per disaster type (flood, earthquake, fire), and the formula — not a black box — is shown on the dashboard beside each marker.
9. Command Center Dashboard
Live merged swarm map with triage-scored survivor markers, hazard zones (each with its ML/deterministic reasoning trace), and a safe-access-route overlay.
Per-drone mission status: battery, comms link quality, current sub-region being swept.
Alert feed separating auto-flagged (high-confidence) from needs-review (disagreement) hazards.
Fully functional offline against locally synced mesh data; upgrades to full imagery once connectivity resumes.

11. Implementation Roadmap
Phase 1 — Proof of concept: Single drone (or ground rig simulating drone sensors) running RGB+thermal fusion detection with the deterministic hazard-rule engine, logging geotags to a local map.
Phase 2: Add acoustic and gas sensing, the triage-scoring engine, and the offline dashboard.
Phase 3: Two-drone mesh demo — decentralized map merge over ESP-NOW/LoRa, with the swarm coverage bias visibly converging on simulated survivor signal sources.
Phase 4 — Stretch: Full GPS-denied navigation fallback demo (dead-reckoning plus thermal optical flow) in a smoke-simulated environment.

12. Tier-2 Novelty Extensions
These extensions push past a conventional multi-sensor drone concept by adding genuinely new sensing or intelligence primitives rather than another camera.
12.1 Through-Rubble Vital-Sign Radar
Micro-Doppler processing on an FMCW/UWB radar return detects the sub-millimeter chest displacement of breathing and heartbeat through non-metallic debris, rubble, and thin concrete or timber — answering the brief's hardest case: a survivor who is buried, silent, and thermally masked.
12.2 Predictive Hazard Propagation
A deterministic, on-device cellular-automaton model projects how a detected hazard evolves over the next 15–60 minutes — fire spread from wind vector and fuel density, or flood rise from terrain gradient and water-level delta — kept separate from the ML stack so its output stays interpretable.
12.3 Active Ultrasonic Void-Echo Mapping
Ultrasonic pulses into collapsed-structure debris fields, analyzed by time-of-flight echo pattern, flag likely void pockets — cavities large enough to shelter a survivor — before any other sensor confirms a person is present.
12.4 Bandwidth-Constrained Federated Field Adaptation
Drones exchange kilobyte-scale model-delta updates over the mesh when in range, so a correction learned by one drone (e.g. local debris color causing thermal false positives) propagates across the swarm without touching the cloud.
12.5 Swarm-Level Bayesian Belief Fusion
Independent detection confidences for the same geotagged area, observed by two or more drones, are combined via a Bayesian update rather than trusting whichever single reading arrived first — collective confirmation computed at the edge.
12.6 Physical Relay-Pod Deployment
Drones air-drop small solar-powered LoRa relay pods at chosen waypoints, physically extending the mesh's footprint in dense urban rubble rather than relying solely on drone-to-drone range.
13. True End-to-End Autonomy Stack
Sensing and intelligence are not the same as autonomy. AEGIS-SAR closes a sense-plan-act loop that runs onboard with no human in the control loop during a mission: a coordinator sets the mission scope, and the swarm handles everything else until it returns or reports.
13.1 Autonomous Mission Loop
Takeoff & self-check — The drone verifies sensor health, GPS lock (or VIO initialization), battery state, and mesh connectivity before departing.
Frontier-based exploration — Each drone autonomously selects the next frontier — the boundary between explored and unexplored space — rather than following pre-set waypoints.
Interrupt-and-investigate — When a detection crosses a confidence threshold mid-sweep, the drone autonomously breaks its search pattern, re-plans a tighter local trajectory to confirm from multiple angles, updates the triage score, then resumes — without waiting for operator approval.
Autonomous obstacle negotiation — The ToF ring and depth camera feed a reactive local planner that re-routes around debris or dynamic hazards at control-loop rate.
Battery/return management — Each drone predicts remaining flight time against distance-to-return and autonomously selects a safe landing spot using deterministic terrain-flatness analysis.
Autonomous report generation — On return, the drone compiles geo-tagged detections, triage scores, and the hazard map into a structured report with no manual annotation.
13.2 Decentralized Autonomous Task Allocation
No central controller assigns search areas. Drones run a lightweight auction algorithm over the mesh — broadcasting position, battery, and coverage gaps — so zones are allocated to whichever drone can cover them most efficiently. A high-triage-score discovery triggers nearby drones to autonomously re-bid toward corroboration or backfilling the abandoned area.
13.3 What Autonomy Level This Targets
AEGIS-SAR targets bounded full autonomy: a human sets mission scope and receives outputs, but does not fly, approve individual maneuvers, or direct search patterns. It does not claim fully unsupervised, open-ended autonomy — a rescue coordinator still defines the mission boundary and retains final say on dispatching ground teams. This is deliberately an honest, defensible autonomy claim.
14. Limitations
Acoustic detection is unreliable in high-wind or high-machinery-noise zones; treated as a confirmatory signal, not a primary one.
RTK GPS range and full swarm mesh range require real-world field testing beyond a hackathon's scope; the roadmap scopes a feasible demo subset.
MOX gas sensors drift and need periodic calibration; deterministic drift compensation reduces but does not eliminate this.
